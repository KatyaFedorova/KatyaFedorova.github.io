Work About

I'm Kat. I build LLM features, and then I work out whether they're actually any good.

Seven years I was a Software Developer in Test — frameworks, pipelines, the unglamorous work of making releases boring. In 2024 I stopped, on purpose, and spent a year retraining into AI engineering and evaluation. I'm two years into that now, and it turns out the two halves fit together better than I expected: most people can get a model to produce something plausible, and comparatively few can tell you whether it's right, or notice the week it quietly stops being right.

So that's the work. I build the feature — provider-agnostic clients, structured outputs, retrieval, agents — and then I build the thing that judges it: a labelled set, a rubric someone else could apply and get the same answer, a job in CI that fails when quality slips. I like the measuring part more than I probably should.

I'm in UTC+8 and work EU/UK hours, currently looking for LLM engineering or evaluation work. My résumé is here as a PDF, and the short version is further down this page.

Where I've worked

  1. Apr 2025 — present

    LLM Engineer · TomatoPaws, a cross-platform desk-break app

    I build and test the Kotlin Multiplatform app end to end. The LLM part generates personalised break suggestions: one LlmClient interface with Claude and OpenAI behind it, switchable by config, falling back automatically when one provider misbehaves. The evaluation came with it — a labelled input set, a rubric scoring relevance, safety, tone and length, side-by-side model comparison, and a CI job that fails the build on regression. Claude Code, Codex CLI and MCP tooling all day.

  2. Apr 2024 — Apr 2025

    A year out, on purpose · self-directed

    I left to retrain properly rather than add "AI" to my title and hope. A year of LLMs, evaluation methods and the awkward problem of testing systems that don't give the same answer twice. Not a gap I'm shy about — it's the reason the rest of this page exists.

  3. Apr 2022 — Apr 2024

Led the automation framework (Playwright, TypeScript, Python), owned the GitLab CI pipelines and quality gates, and was the team's security champion. Also built an internal LLM proof of concept for summarising and prioritising urgent work items, with acceptance criteria and a rubric before anyone was allowed to roll it out.

What I know my way around

Building with LLMs

Claude (Anthropic) API, OpenAI API, AWS Bedrock; prompt design and versioning, structured outputs and JSON schema, function and tool calling, RAG — embeddings, chunking, vector search with pgvector and Qdrant, retrieval evaluation — multi-step agents, MCP servers, provider abstraction with automatic fallback, streaming, caching, and the token, latency and cost arithmetic that decides whether any of it ships.

Evaluation

Eval-set and rubric design, golden datasets, labelling guidelines and inter-annotator agreement, LLM-as-judge calibrated against humans, prompt regression testing, side-by-side comparison and A/B testing, significance and confidence intervals so a "better" model is measurably better, groundedness and hallucination checks, quality gates in CI. Tooling: promptfoo, Ragas, DeepEval, Langfuse and LangSmith.

Safety and reliability

Red-teaming, prompt injection and jailbreak testing, guardrails and refusal behaviour, PII handling, failure-mode analysis, and the general business of testing systems that won't sit still.

Everything else

Python, TypeScript, JavaScript, Kotlin Multiplatform, Java, C#; SQL, FastAPI, Playwright, Git, Docker, GitLab CI, GitHub Actions, API and contract testing, and owning a CI/CD pipeline end to end.
